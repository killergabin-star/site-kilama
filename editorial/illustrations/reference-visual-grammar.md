# Reference visual grammar for Policy illustrations

This note extracts reusable visual logic from premium current-affairs references
without copying their identity, layouts, artists, or proprietary assets.

## What to learn

The references are useful because they treat the image as an editorial argument.
For the Policy section, the thumbnail should not be a decorative theme marker.
It should answer: what mechanism does this article make visible?

## Reference roles

### The Economist

Useful signal: conceptual compression.

- one metaphor per image;
- strong small-size readability;
- restrained palette with a decisive accent;
- editorial irony or tension when the subject permits it;
- image as a claim, not as an ambience.

Use for:

- polycrisis;
- trade and sanctions;
- development-finance contradictions;
- institutional failure;
- strategic chokepoints.

Avoid:

- imitating a named illustrator;
- recreating covers;
- copying exact visual compositions.

### Foreign Affairs

Useful signal: sober symbolic authority.

- more classical, less cartoonish;
- large central object or scene;
- restrained negative space;
- institutional seriousness over visual punch;
- image should age well and not feel like a social-media card.

Use for:

- long reports;
- diplomatic or strategic analysis;
- Iran, G7, China, debt architecture, institutions;
- pieces that need gravity more than wit.

### Financial Times

Useful signal: data-led abstraction.

- the visual form follows the analytical relation;
- flows, ranks, parts-of-whole, divergence, correlations, maps and time paths
  are separate visual problems;
- chart logic can become editorial metaphor when the data itself is the story.

Use for:

- reports with many results;
- macro-financial or trade quantification;
- WEO / Spring Meetings / rearmament cost pieces;
- articles where the figure is more important than a symbolic object.

## Our synthesis

The erickilama.com register is not a copy of any of these. It should sit at the
intersection of:

- Economist-style conceptual recall;
- Foreign Affairs-style strategic sobriety;
- FT-style data discipline;
- the existing site palette: near-black, warm ivory, analytical red, occasional
  muted ochre or blue-grey.

## Editorial rule

Every visual candidate must be scored on three axes before it is promoted:

1. Article fidelity: does it express the specific mechanism of the piece?
2. Series coherence: does it belong to the Policy visual system?
3. Small-size legibility: does it still work at 160 x 90 px?

If any score is weak, the image stays in the lab and does not go live.
